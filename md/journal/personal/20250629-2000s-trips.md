## Elden Journal - (2025.06.29) Going Back to the 00s
Much of the time I like to take what I call 90s or 2000s trips. Believe it or
not, I will leave with minimal technology on my person, or something
functionally equivalent to what I carried in my early teens. Either way, I like
to leave the internet at home, on my real computers that run real programs. I
know every generation says that things peaked in their time, but I genuinely
think consumer tech in general peaked for late Millennials and early Gen-Z; we
got to grow up in an era of tech that left all the same possibilities open as
the last gen and more, was more capable than ever, but above all else it
*existed to serve us.* My iPod didn't show me ads, it just played music. My
computer didn't siphon all my data off to several different ad companies or beg
me to subscribe t0 five different services, it just ran programs. My PSP *did*
try to get me to buy games, but did I need an internet connection to play them?
No.

### 90s Trips
This is where I will leave the house with basically nothing but my wallet,
keys, and gun. I usually do this going to the store, lake, or somewhere else I
am 100% sure I could walk back from, worst case scenario.

### 2000s Trips
I personally prefer these because I did grow up carrying some tech on me beyond
a CD player. My actual 00s tech

#### 2020s PDA
It might be cheating slightly because the device I usefor this was made in
2019, but before I switched to iPhones, I dailied a OnePlus 7T Pro McLaren.
A *very* high-spec phone at the time that is still fantastically usable today.
With a few software enhancements it makes a really nice PDA. While I do own 00s
devices, and could use them today, it's much more practical to use a smartphone
without service. My old devices are also basically relics that I do not want to
put in harm's way.

To start, it does not run OxygenOS anymore. I have it flashed with crDroid x.x
(Android 14) which is only one major release behind the current at the time of
writing; and given how long Android releases are (technically) supported by
Google, this device should last me into the 2030s for this purpose if not
longer. Who knows, I may one day flash Android 20 onto it, replace the battery
and use it into the 2040s.

Next, I yanked out the SIM. It can make 911 calls and that is it. I no longer
use a compatible carrier anyway; it is unlocked, but anything besides T-Mo's
network would limit it to LTE anyway, so why bother? That's a few extra minutes
of offline usefulness or ability to call for help.

Then, instead of flashing Google's services (e.g. the Play Store) I left those
out and installed microG, a privacy-respecting analogue. On top of that I
flashed a Zygisk module to spoof the signatures of those microG modules, so
apps see them as legitimate Google services. That way the few apps that
*demand* their presence (like, obviously, Google Maps) will work anyway and
accept the not-Google analogues.

Then, I set about cramming as much offline functionality into my 256GB of
storage as I could. Entire music library? Downloaded. Basic productivity apps?
Got em. Offline maps of the whole Texoma area? I have those, and GPS doesn't
require cell service. I'm set to go fishing or do whatever, wherever. On top of
that, I have a camera that is still rather bougie by 2025 standards; this thing
takes *real* macro photos of bugs and whatever else I find cool, using its
wide-angle lens and physical focusing functionality. It's still current-gen
tech as far as I'm concerned. I also left plenty of memory free for RAW photos
and high-quality videos.

And that's it. That covers all my 2000s-tech bases:
- media player
- digital camera/camcorder
- GPS unit
- basic productivity (to-do list, calendar, etc)
- access to 911

But *much better* than I had back then, in one convenient device, and with none
of the drawbacks of that device keeping me available, connected, and ripe for
data harvesting 24/7/365. The way tech would have generally evolved in an ideal
world.
